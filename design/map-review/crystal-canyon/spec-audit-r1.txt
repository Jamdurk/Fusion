B. Numbered fixes, priority order

1. Fix terrain seating methodology — REQUIRED

The specification says every asset is raycast-seated on the lowest ground under its footprint. That is risky.

For ordinary rocks and crystal clusters, seat on a representative contact plane using multiple terrain samples. Allow shallow burial, but no floating corners.

For the landmark, prepare a roughly 30 × 28-stud stable foundation on the plateau.

For the arch, sample both bridge abutments independently. Do not use the low channel floor beneath its centre as the seating height.

For the nests, level the alcove footprint before seating the mound.

Your scaled arch is approximately 58 studs across, 25.5 high and 10.8 deep, assuming the 1.7 multiplier applies to height.

The bridge must meet the plateau on both sides and leave at least 12 studs of clear height above the canyon floor. Check this after carving.

2. Widen channel pinch points — REQUIRED

Your previous terrain measurements showed floor widths of only 18–22 studs in several places.

An 18-stud-wide walking route inside an 18-stud-wide canyon has essentially no clearance.

Carve narrow channel-floor sections to 26 studs minimum.

Use 30–32 studs at sharp bends, mining-bay junctions and nest approaches.

Keep the final usable route at least 18 studs after placing decorations.

Verify Link A and Link B remain open after terrain voxelisation.

Do not fill the newly widened sections with decorative outcrops.

3. Correct the lighting budget — REQUIRED

You specified one landmark light, three seam lights and one tunnel light per nest.

That's seven potential dynamic lights, exceeding your six-per-view budget if all are visible.

Change to:

One spire light, shadowless, range 42.

At most two seam lights affecting a busy view, shadowless, range 10–14.

One tunnel light per nearby nest, range 10–12, but enable only the closest two when appropriate.

All other orange glow comes from emissive masks, not PointLights.

Keep a hard ceiling of six active visible local lights; aim for four or fewer in normal exploration.

4. Preserve the spire's crystal hierarchy — HIGH

Seven main crystals could become visually crowded compared with E2b.

Keep the seven, but ensure three or four dominate the silhouette. The remainder should be visibly shorter and partly buried between the main crystals.

Your 6.3k-triangle landmark is excellent. Don't add geometry simply to increase detail.

5. Reduce unnecessary texture memory — HIGH

Nine 1024-pixel texture sets with four maps each can be expensive. Uncompressed RGBA8 equivalents would occupy roughly 144 MiB before mipmaps, although actual compressed and runtime memory use varies.

I recommend:

1024: landmark crystals, main basalt, prominent seam and nest shell.

512: secondary clusters, columns, common rocks and ground shards.

256: tunnel lining and minor reusable details.

Reuse identical MeshIds and texture IDs across repeated objects.

Baking at 2× resolution is fine if the exported textures remain at the intended sizes.

6. Control the basalt emission — MEDIUM

Your lighter basalt values are justified by the previous Studio experience. I would not force the stone darker.

The important rule is that ordinary basalt remains predominantly non-emissive, even with EmissiveStrength around 1.4.

Keep approximately 70–80% of the ordinary rock surface black in its emission mask. The strongest glow should belong to crystal roots, selected fractures and magma interiors.

Roblox multiplies emissive contribution by the surface colour, and excessive values can clamp toward white.

D. Final implementation checks

Three smaller but important issues to carry into Studio:

Mining collision: Decorative crystals can remain CanCollide = false, but ensure vein hitboxes remain queryable so mining raycasts work.

Arch collision: Use simple invisible collision geometry for the walkable deck and sides, rather than relying on detailed per-block mesh collisions.

LOD and draw calls: Keep crystals and basalt as reusable assets. Roblox specifically identifies lost mesh instancing, excessive mesh density and overlapping transparent effects as performance risks.
