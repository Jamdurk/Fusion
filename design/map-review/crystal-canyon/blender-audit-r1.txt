B. Numbered fixes
1. Rework the crystal nest tunnel — CRITICAL

The concentric rings and dark central dot make the entrance resemble a mechanical target or giant eyeball.

Exact changes:

Keep the nest's 34 × 24 × 17 overall size.

Increase visible tunnel-mouth width from 9 to approximately 11 studs.

Replace perfectly circular rings with 6–8 irregular angular basalt ribs.

Make the opening slightly wider at the bottom than the top.

Darken the tunnel centre substantially; don't put a bright circular emission disc at the back.

Concentrate orange emission along the inside walls, with an uneven glow increasing deeper into the tunnel.

Preserve approximately 8–11 studs of visible interior depth.

The opening should look like a passage into glowing rock, not a portal painted onto a wall.

Also, the long magma plate extending directly from the nest resembles a manufactured runway. Shorten that treatment to approximately 6–10 studs immediately outside the mouth, unless the longer seam has a deliberate gameplay purpose.

2. Restore the amber crystal depth — HIGH

The crystals are currently predominantly pale yellow, particularly in the spire and larger clusters.

The Standard view transform explains some difference, but I wouldn't assume it explains everything.

Make the darker orange root colour occupy approximately 25–35% of the visible crystal length.

Introduce larger, faint internal veils and irregular fracture planes into the colour and normal bakes. The close-up currently reads too homogeneous.

3. Make basalt columns look geological — HIGH

The two column outcrops currently resemble stacked rectangular architectural blocks rather than naturally fractured columnar basalt.

Exact changes:

Use recognisable five- to seven-sided column profiles.

Vary neighbouring column heights approximately ±20%.

Rotate individual columns around their vertical axes by 5–12°.

Tilt two or three columns approximately 4–8°.

Break or chip at least 20% of the column tops.

Make horizontal joints less perfectly aligned.

Remove roughly 30% of their straight glowing vertical seams.

Keep the original 24- and 15-stud heights as approximate overall envelopes.

This should be a geometry adjustment, not simply a texture rebake.

4. Strengthen the mineable DNA vein — MEDIUM

The current vein looks too similar to ordinary decorative clusters.

Make one main core crystal approximately 1.4× larger than the current dominant crystal.

Expose a bright amber root fracture beneath it.

Add 3–5 small broken crystal pieces around the base.

Arrange surrounding rock into a chipped-out cavity, as E6 illustrates.

Target: players should distinguish a mineable node from a decorative crystal cluster without needing a UI label.

5. Integrate seam plates and improve the arch — MEDIUM

The seam plates resemble manufactured rectangular strips when isolated.

Give both variants irregular, chipped boundaries.

Add a 1–2-stud visually scorched margin around the active fissures.

Bury the plate perimeter slightly when placed.

Keep the most luminous cracks narrow and discontinuous.

For the arch, vary the height and angle of its supporting blocks. Keep the 24-stud opening unobstructed and ensure its scaled walkable deck meets the plateau safely.

Avoid excessive glowing cracks on every boulder. Unbroken mid-grey basalt should remain the dominant background material.

C. What I would keep unchanged

The spire's basic silhouette, cluster sizes, shard kit, overall triangle counts and lighter basalt albedo are all acceptable.

Do not darken all basalt just to match the cinematic reference. Your previous Studio experience with near-black stone justifies the lighter material. What matters is preserving visible carved texture under real moon lighting.

I also wouldn't add more crystal polygons. The existing geometries have sufficient detail; the issue is how their surfaces are shaded.
