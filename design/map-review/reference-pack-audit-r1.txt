Emissive pieces means individual Neon or similar visible elements, not PointLights. Emitters means concurrently active emitters, not total emitters stored in the world.

Hub targets: Aim for 150–250k environment triangles rendered in a typical view, with preferably fewer than 500–700 scene draw calls. Reserve extra headroom for players and aliens. Keep around 4–6 actual dynamic light sources affecting a busy view, with shadows disabled for decorative lights. These are intentionally conservative starting targets.

For the battle arena, reserve substantial rendering and CPU capacity for the 64-unit army simulation rather than using the entire budget on terrain decoration.

Mandatory simplifications

Trees: 600–1,800 triangles each. Use 3–5 opaque canopy masses, not thousands of tiny leaves. Reuse meshes across the forest.

Mushrooms and goo: Mostly opaque meshes, with one thin visible goo surface per pool. Do not stack transparent layers to create depth.

Crystals: Faceted meshes using roughly 100–700 triangles per cluster. Use selective Neon faces or separate inset emissive geometry, not a PointLight inside every crystal.

Machines: Share metal trims, materials and conveyor sections. Each major machine should generally cost about 600–3,000 triangles, depending on importance.

Incubator: One transparent dome, not several nested glass shells. The egg itself can be opaque and emissive.

Particles: Pool small bursts. Prefer a handful of large, readable spores or floating debris over hundreds of tiny particles.

Textures: Use shared trim sheets and materials, generally 256–512 px for minor environmental assets. Reuse identical uploaded MeshIds so Roblox can instance repeated assets efficiently.
