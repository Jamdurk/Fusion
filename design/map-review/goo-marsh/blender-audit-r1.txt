B. Required fixes, priority order
1. Goo cap material — HIGH

The large pale spots on the cap are still suspiciously close to the shiny-plastic look James rejected.

They're softer than before, but they look like broad painted highlights rather than glowing material suspended beneath jelly.

Exact fix:

Reduce the brightness of the largest cap spots by approximately 25–35%.

Darken the thick central cap body toward D3's deep teal #0A6B8F, blended with cyan #31D9FF.

Make the embedded spots smaller and less uniformly distributed.

Add tiny internal bubble clusters, mostly within the darker cyan regions.

Concentrate pale wet sheen along the rolled rim rather than the whole dome.

Target roughness approximately 0.35–0.42 on the body, 0.20–0.27 along the rim.

The cap needs dark interior depth + bright thin edges, not an evenly illuminated sky-blue surface.

2. Pools — HIGH, biggest material issue

The pale branching lines across both pools still read as cracked ice.

D3's pools have gentle ripples, visible depth and smooth flowing surfaces.

Exact fix:

Remove approximately 80–90% of the branching crack pattern.

Replace it with 2–4 broad, low-contrast curved ripples per pool.

Make the centre visibly darker teal than the outer 3–5 studs.

Preserve a soft luminous cyan rim, approximately 0.5–1.2 studs wide.

Add a few suspended bubbles and subtle internal colour variation.

Make the thin films less uniformly bright than the pool edges.

The pools should read as viscous liquid even without animation.

3. Nest — HIGH

The doorway is already being fixed, which is necessary.

The current shell also looks too much like an opaque, cyan plastic dome with little knobs.

Exact fix:

Widen the opening to at least 8 studs, approximately 7 studs high.

Give the opening a rounded, irregular 2–3-stud-thick goo lip.

Show at least 5–6 studs of visible tunnel depth with a violet-to-dark-purple gradient.

Darken the thick dome body by 20–25%, keeping its thin edges cyan.

Make the horns and bumps asymmetric, with 2–3 visibly slumped into the base.

The nest should resemble an enormous living jelly deposit with a tunnel through it, not a manufactured object.

4. Stalk transition — MEDIUM

The twist and root flare are good, but there's a conspicuous abrupt change between the grey-lilac upper trunk and the almost white lower roots.

Exact fix:

Blend that colour transition over approximately 6–8 studs vertically.

Keep the stalk predominantly pearl-white.

Use pale lavender-grey only inside the twisting grooves.

Preserve directional grain and the existing root geometry.

The current two-tone effect makes the trunk look assembled from different materials.

5. Gill underside — MEDIUM

The gills themselves are good. Keep the 14 broad blades.

However, in the direct-below view, the central exposed structure looks like a spiky mechanical star.

Reduce the protruding central geometry by approximately 40%.

Make the root of the blades transition smoothly into the stalk.

Keep stronger pink-violet light deep between blades.

Remove or greatly soften the regularly spaced cyan dots around the outer underside rim.

The goal is organic radial growth, not a turbine.

6. DNA vein and small goo pieces — MEDIUM

The small rock-and-crystal assets almost disappear against the studio-grey background.

Darken the basalt to a visibly charcoal-grey value.

Give the main cyan crystals a clearer 4–6-stud vertical silhouette.

Concentrate glow near the crystal bases and central fracture.

Increase the visual irregularity of the blob mound, especially where it spreads onto the ground.

The current blob shapes are better than spheres, but the largest still needs a more convincing sagging edge.
