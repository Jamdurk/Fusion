C. Required changes, most important first

1. Reduce secondary-mushroom geometry — HIGH

Your current counts imply approximately 247–251k placed triangles. The largest avoidable cost is:

30 trumpet clusters × 2,200 = 66k triangles.

28 umbrellas × 2,000 = 56k triangles.

That's 122k triangles in secondary mushrooms alone.

Change: Reduce each trumpet cluster to 1,200–1,500 triangles and each umbrella to 900–1,200. Keep the three giant mushrooms at 8.5k each.

Target 175–200k placed triangles for the finished marsh, and approximately 40–65k rendered marsh triangles per view on the baseline phone.

Do not reduce the mushroom counts yet.

2. Increase the actual goo coverage — HIGH

Six 48-stud pools and ten narrow streams cover only a small fraction of a 340 × 660 basin.

Without additional surface treatment, you risk building a grey moon valley containing mushrooms instead of a Goo Marsh.

Keep the six main pools, but add:

Two wider pools or shallow extensions, approximately 70–85 studs across.

10–14 irregular thin-film goo patches, roughly 20–40 studs long.

Goo-coated banks and connecting trickles around the existing pools.

Aim for approximately 10–15% of the biome visibly affected by goo, without covering every walking route. Use opaque meshes and baked detail.

3. Correct the giant gill specification — MEDIUM

D2b shows approximately 12 broad readable radial gills, but your specification uses 64 modelled blades.

64 equally prominent blades will look too finely ribbed at phone distance.

Use 12–16 primary gill blades, with finer secondary grooves baked into the normal map. Keep the deep lilac emission between them. This preserves D2b's chunky silhouette.

4. Move the entrance landmark slightly deeper — HIGH

The giant at (-511, -35) has a 40-stud-wide cap. Its edge comes almost to the biome boundary and may interfere with your X −535 route.

Move it to approximately X −575, Z −35, then verify a minimum 18-stud clear walking corridor.

Keep one giant clearly visible from each western plot, but don't let its stalk or hanging drips obstruct the entrance.

5. Fix particle and lighting budgets — HIGH

Starting every emitter at Rate 30 is excessive for a calm marsh, particularly with multiple pools and mushroom caps.

Roblox specifically warns that overlapping particles cause GPU overdraw, with mobile devices having lower particle limits. 
GitHub

Use:

Drips: approximately 1–3 particles/second on nearby giant mushrooms only.

Pool bubbles: 1–2 particles/second on selected pools.

Spores: 1 particle/second or occasional bursts.

Total target: roughly 25–60 simultaneously visible particles on a low-end phone.

Dynamic lights: only the nearest 1–2 giant caps and, where useful, one nearby nest; all shadowless.

6. Optimise the texture bake and emissive masks — HIGH

Do not automatically create four unique 1024-pixel textures for every individual mesh.

Giant cap, gills and stalk: 1024 maps where visibly justified.

Nests: 512–1024.

Secondary mushrooms and blobs: shared 512 atlases.

Small rocks and sprouts: 256–512.

Reuse the same mesh and texture assets across instances.

Your use of EmissiveMaskContent is supported by Roblox. However, its contribution is multiplied by albedo, and overly high EmissiveStrength can clamp bright regions towards white. Keep the bright spots restrained.

Also, Blender's Ambient Occlusion node with Inside enabled is a useful approximation for the jelly shading, but it measures internal occlusion rather than literal material thickness. Validate the baked gradient on the giant cap and one slumped blob before batch generation.
