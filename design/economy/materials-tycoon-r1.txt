The most important lesson is that successful progression games make the player understand what their next purchase or discovery will accomplish.

For Alien Fusion, every major upgrade should visibly do at least one of three things: build a more impressive base, hatch more aliens, or help conquer the next fort.

2. The three materials I'd choose

Rather than invent a separate third currency, split the existing ALIEN DNA into two family-specific resources.

1. MOON ROCK
Build your base

Grey rocks mined on your plot and across the moon. Drills generate it automatically; forts and mining events give bonuses.

Spend on: purchase pads, drills, refinery, pickaxes, incubator upgrades, launch-pad upgrades and decorations.

2. GOO DNA
Hatch Squish aliens

Bright cyan material mined mainly in the Goo Marsh, found in Moonwood pods, earned from Goo nests and occasional meteorites.

Spend on: Squish eggs, producing Drips that can become Twins, Brutes and Titans.

3. CRYSTAL DNA
Hatch Crunch aliens

Glowing amber material mined in Crystal Canyon, earned from Crystal nests, found in meteorites and later collected by crystal extractors.

Spend on: Crunch eggs, producing Pebbles that can become Pincers, Rams and Colossi.

What about rare aliens?

Keep rarity tied to the egg's rolled appearance, not a fourth resource.

A Goo Egg always hatches a Drip. Its appearance might be Normal, Glowing, Crystal, Shadow, Rainbow or Cosmic.

A Crystal Egg always hatches a Pebble, with the same cosmetic rarity system.

Merging doesn't cost DNA. The actual aliens are preserved through the merge and split, including their individual looks. This protects the locked 32-matter system and avoids charging players for tactical decisions.

When interacting with the Incubator, the UI should simply show two eggs: SQUISH EGG and CRUNCH EGG, each displaying its DNA cost and hatch timer.

The player chooses which army to grow. Every egg produces one uniquely owned, one-matter alien. The existing army cap, cosmetic rarities and fusion rules remain.

What should the machines do while players are away?

Moon Rock drills continue earning through the existing capped offline-income calculation. Goo and Crystal Extractors can also generate a small offline amount, but active mining, meteorites and conquering forts should remain the faster ways to obtain DNA.

I'd avoid a complicated system where players must repeatedly convert raw ore into bars, bars into DNA, DNA into energy and energy into eggs. One direct conversion is enough: DNA → Egg → Alien.

4. What the player experiences
1 min

"I'm building an alien base!"

The player claims a plot, mines Moon Rock, buys their first drill and sees eight starter Drips. The launch pad introduces an easy enemy nest, with the next objective clearly visible.

10 min

"I have a growing army!"

They've used the Incubator, discovered a cosmetic mutation, reached their first Titan-capable army and learned Orders. Fort 1 has been conquered or is an immediate goal. Crystal Canyon offers the first Crunch egg, while Fort 2 is visible farther ahead.

2 hours

"This is MY alien empire!"

Their base has multiple working machines, two developing alien collections, rare cosmetic variants and conquered forts. They're pursuing better mastery, repeatable fort challenges and the King of the Moon, while earning resources for further upgrades.

The earlier targets of 16 owned matter in 5–8 minutes and 32 in 20–30 minutes can remain the initial tuning goals.

Crucially, there should never be a long period where players are only mining while waiting for eggs. They should be able to build, explore, fight, collect or show off something.
