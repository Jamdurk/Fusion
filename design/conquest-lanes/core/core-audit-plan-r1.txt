Wild: Violet lower core, cyan upper orb, neutral shield pylons. One 32-matter NPC garrison appears when a conquest begins.

Held: Owner-coloured light strips, core glow, shield links and banners. The same reactor geometry remains, now defended by the holder's 32-matter garrison.

Combat sequence

Challenger enters — 0–3 seconds

The reactor produces a low energy surge. The two pylons light up, a shield pattern appears over the core, and the 32-matter garrison deploys once.

Destroy the shield pylons — 400 HP each

Both pylons can be attacked in either order. Each destroyed pylon breaks its own energy link with a brief flash. No reinforcements spawn.

Shields collapse — 1.5-second transition

When both pylons are destroyed, the shield folds inward and disappears. The lower core is now vulnerable. Give it a clear exposed-target indicator.

Destroy the reactor core — 3,000 HP

The violet core pulses faster as HP falls. At zero HP, the ring briefly slows, beams cut out, and a contained energy shockwave spreads across the scar.

Conquest — 3–5-second transformation

The reactor restarts in the winner's colour, banners rise, the upper orb changes accent colour, and a large crown hologram appears above the ring.

Important: Don't destroy the crashed shell, launch physical debris or move the collision geometry during a raid. Use visual animation and colour changes.

If the challenger loses, the owner retains the throne and it resets for the next attempt. Each attempt gets one finite garrison deployment, not an endless spawning loop.

For the first implementation, allow one active throne assault per server to avoid eight competing battles trying to change one shared reactor state. Other players can observe. All HP, capture and army outcomes remain server-authoritative.

C. Containment

Don't build a continuous solid energy wall. It would hide the wreck and interfere with the eight attack routes.

Instead, use the crater's basalt rim as the visible boundary, with occasional short rock buttresses and a very faint translucent energy arc between the entrance points during combat.

Containment should be enforced by the server-side army navigation area, not by relying on visual barriers. Keep defenders inside the radius-72 combat boundary and only allow attackers to enter through their assigned lane approach.

5. Eight lane entrances — preserve the crater

Your eight lanes arrive at radius 125, but the existing natural breaches are mostly east/west, while the lanes are at 22.5° increments.

Recommendation: eight shallow, terrain-cut entry ramps, one for each lane.

Start around radius 125.

Cut narrow passages through the rim around radius 80–90.

End at prepared inner shelves around radius 68–72.

Keep 18–20 studs clear, with 10–12-stud soft side transitions where possible.

Test the actual gradient with F4 units. Don't force a steep descent simply to preserve the rim height.

Leave substantial raised basalt between entrances, so the crater still has an unmistakable broken-rim silhouette.

Keep the existing low breaches and southwest notch as geological features. Avoid flattening the entire rim to create eight entrances.

The reactor's tall silhouette must remain readable from all eight plots.

Start with one combat terrace, one shield pylon and one entry ramp using simple greybox parts and terrain. Test a 32-matter attack against the 32-matter garrison to confirm there's space around the shell.
